How to Stop AI Hallucinations in Financial & Enterprise Analysis

Why frontier LLMs fabricate citations and market figures during complex synthesis, and how a four-stage verification pipeline (EVES) reduces fabricated claims to 9.7% on 432 factual questions.

Published 2026-10-21 · 7 min read · By Eden (Harrat Global Holdings, Inc.)
Explore EVES See the 432-question benchmark

When teams use large language models to draft financial analyses, competitive teardowns, or due-diligence briefs, they run into a predictable failure mode: models optimize for plausible continuation, not epistemic certainty. Ask a single model from memory for a private company’s net dollar retention or a sub-sector’s 5-year CAGR, and it will often state an unverified number with total confidence.

The three root causes of hallucinations in enterprise synthesis

  1. Parametric compression loss: Even frontier models compress trillions of training tokens into weights. Exact figures, dates, citations, and minority edge cases blur together unless grounded at inference time.
  2. Sycophantic premise acceptance: If a prompt contains a mistaken assumption (“Given that Company Y’s gross margin fell in 2025, explain why…”), standard instruction-tuned models try to satisfy the user’s premise rather than challenge it.
  3. Single-model blind spots: No single provider dominates every domain. A model chosen for low cost on routine summarization may have a >25% fabrication rate on dense factual recall.

A four-stage architecture for zero-trust synthesis

In EVES (Eden’s verification and evaluation system), we found that stopping hallucinations requires intervening both before and after generation:

STAGE 1 · RISK-AWARE ROUTING

Route by factual risk, not just token length

Eden Router scores every prompt for factual risk (medical, legal, financial, citations, named entities) and shifts risky prompts toward higher-reliability models and higher reasoning effort.

STAGE 2 · PRE-GENERATION SEARCH

Ground volatile facts before drafting

When a prompt asks for specific figures, filings, or events, EVES runs targeted web retrieval first so the generator synthesizes from retrieved evidence rather than memory.

STAGE 3 · PREMISE VERIFICATION

Audit the prompt’s assumptions

Before answering a “why” or “how much” question, verify that the underlying entity, event, or directional trend in the question actually occurred.

STAGE 4 · POST-ANSWER CLAIM AUDIT

Verify atomic claims & allow abstention

Check factual claims in the draft against independent sources afterwards. When sources conflict or don’t exist, explicitly say so instead of guessing.

Benchmark evidence: 432 factual & false-premise questions

Across 300 SimpleQA Verified questions and 132 false-premise questions, EVES stated something false 9.7% of the time (95% CI: 7.3%–12.9%), cutting hallucinations by more than half compared to Claude Sonnet 5.5 (24.5%) and Gemini 3.8 Flash (21.8%) answering from memory. Frequently asked questions

Why does RAG alone still hallucinate in financial analysis?

Standard RAG retrieves top-k chunks by embedding similarity, even when none of the chunks actually answer the question or when the user’s prompt contains a false premise. Without premise auditing and post-generation claim verification, models still extrapolate beyond the retrieved text.

How much does verification reduce false-premise hallucinations?

On 132 false-premise questions, EVES made up an answer only 3.0% of the time (vs. 10.6% for Gemini 3.8 Flash).
